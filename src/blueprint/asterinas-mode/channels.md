# Channels

*Cuts across the resources. Specifies how a kernelet talks to the host and to other kernelets: the vsock device model, its host-side switch, and the copy path. Discharges invariant I2 for the memory that moves between kernelets and I4 for what the host remembers about a connection.*

A kernelet has no shared memory with anyone. It talks to the host and to other kernelets the way a microVM guest does: over `AF_VSOCK`, with the kernel proper's own virtio-vsock driver (checked on the tree: `kernel/core/comps/virtio/src/device/socket`, 865 lines) and its own vsock socket layer (checked: `kernel/core/src/net/socket/vsock`), on a virtual device whose model in the endovisor is a **vsock switch**. What a tenant gets is a socket family it already uses, with the host at CID 2 and each kernelet at a CID of its own; what the design gets is one mechanism for the runtime's control connection, for console and log streams that want a socket rather than a device, and for kernelet-to-kernelet traffic, all of it through the same owner-checked device path as every other device.

## The device

The kernelet runtime attaches one `virtio-vsock` device to a kernelet with `KERNELET_ATTACH` ([endovisor](endovisor.md)); a kernelet without one has no `/dev/vsock` and no channel but its console. The kernelet's **CID** is a `u32` in `[3, u32::MAX)`, allocated at `KERNELET_CREATE` from a host-wide counter that is never reused while the host runs, so that a CID a peer cached can never reach a successor; 0, 1 (`VMADDR_CID_LOCAL`) and 2 (the host) are reserved, and `u32::MAX` is `VMADDR_CID_ANY` (checked on the tree: `vsock/addr.rs`). The driver reads the CID from the device configuration space at offset 256 as two `u32`s (checked: `device/socket/config.rs`), which the model serves. The device has the standard three queues, receive, transmit and event (checked: the driver registers callbacks on all three). The driver posts one 4 KiB buffer on the event queue at initialization and the socket layer, on a `TransportReset` event, resets every connection and reloads the CID (checked: `device/socket/queue.rs`, `vsock/transport/space.rs`); the model accepts that buffer and never completes it, because a kernelet's CID never changes. The driver is unchanged. The device model is the one [Devices](virtualizing-ostd/devices.md) describes, with the vsock packet format of the specification: a header of source and destination CID and port, length, type, operation, flags, and the two credit fields, followed by the payload; the driver's `VirtioVsockHdr` and its operations `Request`, `Response`, `Rst`, `Shutdown`, `Rw`, `CreditUpdate` and `CreditRequest` are exactly those (checked on the tree: `device/socket/header.rs`). Only stream sockets are negotiated (`VIRTIO_VSOCK_F_STREAM`; checked: `config.rs`).

**The receiver's contract.** The switch interoperates with the tree's socket layer, whose behavior fixes what the switch must do (checked on the tree: `vsock/transport/mod.rs`, `connection/mod.rs`, `send.rs`): it advertises `buf_alloc` of 256 KiB in every header; it sends `CreditUpdate` after consuming a quarter of that; it sends `CreditRequest` when its view of the peer's credit reaches zero and then blocks the sender until a `CreditUpdate` arrives; and it resets a connection whose queued bytes would exceed its 256 KiB. Its receive buffers are 4096 bytes including the 44-byte header, 64 of them, and its transmit packets carry at most 4052 payload bytes (checked: `device/socket/buffer.rs`, `packet.rs`, `queue.rs`). Its connect timeout is 2 seconds and its close timeout 8 seconds.

## The switch

Behind the model sits the switch, one per host, in the endovisor. It holds a table of **connections**, each keyed by the four-tuple of CIDs and ports and holding: the two endpoints, each a kernelet's CID or the host; the per-direction credit; and a bounded queue of payload bytes in flight, in host memory, charged with `charge_host_bytes` to the sending kernelet, or, on a host-endpoint connection, to the kernelet at its other end in both directions. The number of connections a kernelet may hold is bounded by the endovisor's policy (256 by default, chosen), each entry charged to it; a `Request` beyond the bound is answered with `Rst`. The switch does three things:

- **Routes.** A packet a kernelet transmits, taken from its transmit queue by *its* device thread, has its source CID overwritten with the sender's own, since the header is the tenant's kernel's to write and a forged source would let one sandbox speak as another or as the host; it is then delivered by destination CID: to the host endpoint if the CID is 2, to another kernelet's receive queue if the CID is a live kernelet's and the policy allows the pair, and answered with `Rst` otherwise, including the sender's own CID, CID 1, and a CID marked dead. Connection setup (`Request`, `Response`) and teardown (`Shutdown`, `Rst`) are forwarded per the specification.
- **Copies, and enforces credit.** The payload of an `Rw` packet is copied out of the sender's transmit buffer into a host queue entry by the sender's device thread, which then completes the transmit descriptor; the switch wakes the *receiver's* device thread, which pops one of the receive chains the receiver's driver has posted, copies from the entry into it, re-segmented to the receiver's buffer length so that no packet exceeds one receive chain, writes the used ring, and raises the receiver's line. Each copy runs under `guest_memory` on the respective kernelet with the grains pinned: two copies per packet between kernelets, one on the kernelet side between a kernelet and the host plus the endpoint's copy to user space. The host queue is bounded by the credit the receiver advertised, which the switch **caps at a policy value** (64 KiB by default, chosen) by rewriting `buf_alloc` to the smaller of the advertised value and the cap in every header it forwards, and by answering with `Rst` a sender whose bytes in flight exceed that credit. Rewriting is what makes the bound the protocol's rather than a stall: a sender that honored the 256 KiB the tree advertises would otherwise put four times the cap in flight, and refusing to drain its transmit queue would stall every connection on that device, including the runtime's. The cap is a throughput limit, 64 KiB per round trip through two device threads, below what the tree's own window allows, and is to be tuned against it.
- **Resets.** When a kernelet dies, every connection with it as an endpoint is torn down: the entries are marked dead and the CID with them in `on_dying`, which sleeps nowhere and touches no peer's memory, and each peer's device thread, woken, delivers `Rst` into that peer's receive queue, frees and uncharges the queued bytes and removes the entry. The switch table is one of the host-wide tables that name a kernelet, and `destroy` asserts it holds no entry for the id ([Faults, termination, and reclamation](faults-and-reclamation.md)).

**The host endpoint.** A connection to CID 2 has the switch itself as the peer, so the switch originates that side's credit: `buf_alloc` is the endpoint's bound, `fwd_cnt` its read counter, a `CreditRequest` is answered at once, and a `CreditUpdate` is sent whenever a read frees a quarter of the bound, which is what keeps the tree's sender from blocking. The connection surfaces in host user space as an endpoint descriptor obtained through the [endovisor ABI](endovisor.md): `KERNELET_VSOCK_LISTEN` on a sandbox descriptor listens on a host port scoped to that sandbox, not a host-wide port space, for one connection from the kernelet; `KERNELET_VSOCK_CONNECT` sends a `Request` to the kernelet's port, which the switch holds until the kernelet's receive queue is ready and which the tenant's kernel answers with `Rst` if nothing listens (checked on the tree: `space.rs`), under the caller's own timeout. The runtime therefore listens and lets its agent dial out ([kernelet runtime](kernelet-runtime.md)). The descriptor's stream semantics are: a peer `Shutdown` with the send flag is end-of-file on read; a peer `Rst` is `ECONNRESET` on the next read or write; closing the descriptor sends `Shutdown` with both flags and, if the peer has not reset within the tree's 8-second close timeout, `Rst`; `KERNELET_VSOCK_SHUTDOWN` on the descriptor sends a send-side `Shutdown` for a half-close.

The host kernel has an `AF_VSOCK` layer of its own, but it is a guest's: bound at initialization to the single `Virtio-Vsock` device if one exists, with no transport abstraction, and every socket operation fails with `ENODEV` without it (checked on the tree: `vsock/transport/mod.rs`, `init`). If the host itself runs under a hypervisor, that layer talks to the hypervisor, and a kernelet's CID 2 is the endovisor, not the machine's host. The endovisor does not add a second transport to it; it hands out endpoint descriptors backed by the switch, which is what the runtime needs and no more.

## What crosses, and what the host remembers

Nothing but bytes crosses between kernelets: no frame changes owner, no pointer is carried, and the driver on each side sees a device that delivered or accepted a packet. The switch's table holds CIDs, ports, credits and byte queues, never a window address or a task name, so a kernelet's death leaves nothing dangling (invariant I4), and the owner checks of `guest_memory` on both copies are what keep one kernelet's descriptor from naming another's frame (invariant I2).

## The ownership-transfer extension

The two copies per packet are the cost of not sharing memory. The booted prototype moved ownership of a buffer between instances instead, at 104, 177 and 94 cycles to create, send and destroy a reference, and a 4-byte round trip took 9.4 µs against 36.9 µs for virtio-vsock through a microVM (measured on the booted prototype). In this design the same thing would be a **frame move**: the switch, instead of copying a payload, takes a whole grain out of the sender's grant and appends it to the receiver's, rewriting the owner array and both grant tables and mapping it into the receiver's physical window, after which the receiver's driver finds the packet in memory it now owns. It needs a payload that fills a grain, a mapping-count check on the sender's side so that a frame the sender still maps to a process is never moved, and a `JOB_GRANT`-like notification on the receiver's side; and it changes the memory-only-grows assumption (register A1) for the sender. It is left out of the first version and listed for the Limitations chapter, with the copy path as the baseline it must beat. The second version halves the copy path instead: [Zero-copy I/O](zero-copy-io.md) copies once, frame to frame, in the sender's notify hook, and sets the bar a frame move must clear from measured costs: about 3–4 µs to unmap a grain with a shootdown (estimated from a 4 KiB measurement) against 156 µs measured to copy it cold.

## What a tenant sees

- `AF_VSOCK` stream sockets, and only streams: `SOCK_DGRAM` and `SOCK_SEQPACKET` fail, as on the host kernel today.
- The kernelet's CID, the host at CID 2, and connections to other sandboxes by their CIDs if the endovisor's policy allows the pair; a connection to its own CID or to CID 1 is refused, since the tree's socket layer has no loopback.
- When a peer sandbox dies its connections are reset; the tree's socket layer surfaces a reset in the connected state as hang-up and end-of-file, not as an error (checked on the tree: `connection/mod.rs`, `do_rst`).
- Throughput is bounded by the switch's credit cap per connection; latency is two copies and two device-thread hops per packet between kernelets, one of each between a kernelet and the host, which the Evaluation chapter measures against virtio-vsock in a microVM.

## Costs

- Per packet: one or two copies under `guest_memory`, one device-thread wakeup per hop, one `raise_irq` and one worker wakeup at the receiver, plus the endpoint's copy and wakeup for a host endpoint; the switch's table lookups and one header rewrite.
- Per connection: one table entry and a bounded host queue, charged.
- Per kernelet death: one `Rst` per connection, delivered by each peer's device thread, and the queue frees.

## What this page decides

- **All communication is vsock over the ordinary device path** (register D37). The alternative, a dedicated cross-kernelet channel with its own service calls, would be a second mechanism to secure and a second thing for tenants to learn; vsock is what their software already speaks.
- **The switch copies in the first version** (register D38); the frame move is the extension, with its preconditions stated, because it changes the ownership model and the memory-growth assumption and must be measured against the copy path before it is adopted.
- **Host-side vsock endpoints are stream descriptors from the endovisor ABI, not a second transport for the host's `AF_VSOCK` layer** (register D39); teaching the host's guest-side socket layer to route to a switch as well as to a device is more surface than the runtime needs.
- **The switch rewrites advertised credit down to a cap and resets a sender that exceeds it** (register D51). The alternative, back-pressure by leaving the sender's transmit queue undrained, stalls every connection on the device, the runtime's control connection included.
